# HMW framework anchors

Use these sources as a compact reference map. Prefer the primary source linked for each claim. Browse the source when the user requests an exact quote, disputed attribution, or current publication details.

## Min Basadur and Creative Problem Solving

Basadur connects the phrase to Creative Problem Solving work influenced by Sid Parnes and Alex Osborn, and to its application at Procter & Gamble. The relevant lesson is problem definition: separate facts from assumptions, include affected people, defer judgment during divergence, and turn negative challenges into constructive questions.

- [The Origin of How Might We](https://www.basadur.com/the-origin-of-how-might-we/)
- [Basadur Thinking Tools](https://www.basadur.com/basadur-thinking-tools/)

Do not state that Basadur was the sole inventor without qualification; the historical lineage is more nuanced.

## Stanford d.school

The d.school treats HMW questions as actionable provocations that turn a perspective into an ideation prompt. Its worksheet suggests reframing through amplification, opposites, extremes, assumptions, analogy, emotion, and focus. The Design Thinking Bootleg describes Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test as modes rather than a rigid linear protocol.

- [How Might We Questions](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/how-might-we-questions)
- [Design Thinking Bootleg](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/design-thinking-bootleg)

## IDEO Human-Centered Design

IDEO.org places HMW in the move from insight synthesis to ideation. A good question should allow a variety of answers while remaining narrow enough to orient a brainstorm. HMW should follow learning from the people affected by the design challenge.

- [IDEO Design Kit: How Might We](https://www.designkit.org/methods/how-might-we.html)
- [IDEO Design Kit: Human-Centered Design](https://www.designkit.org/human-centered-design.html)

## Design Council Double Diamond

The Double Diamond names four broad phases: Discover, Define, Develop, and Deliver. HMW is most useful near the transition from Define to Develop, after the team has explored the problem and synthesized a challenge, but before it narrows to one solution. The model is a visual description, not a rigid recipe.

- [Design Council Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/)

## Jake Knapp and Design Sprints

The design-sprint lens compresses framing, sketching, prototyping, and customer testing around a consequential question. Use HMW to sharpen the question a sprint should answer; do not represent the HMW as the sprint outcome. The output is evidence and a decision, not production software.

- [Jake Knapp: Sprint](https://jakeknapp.com/sprint)

## Teresa Torres and Opportunity Solution Trees

Torres provides the strongest product-discovery guardrail for HMW work. Connect a desired outcome to evidence-backed opportunities, compare multiple solutions, identify assumptions, and test the riskiest assumptions. Do not invent an opportunity from internal preference or turn an HMW directly into a feature backlog.

- [Opportunity Solution Trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)
- [Assumption Testing](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/)

## Kees Dorst and design framing

Dorst’s design-research work treats framing and frame creation as core design practices and describes design reasoning through abduction. Use this lens when the user asks for a research-level explanation: HMW is a compact way to make a provisional frame explicit, not a neutral restatement of an objective problem.

- [The Core of “Design Thinking” and Its Application](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2011.07.006)
- [Framing in Design: A Formal Analysis and Failure Modes](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/37717/framing_in_design_a_formal_analysis_and_failure_modes)

## Application rule

When referencing an expert, distinguish:

1. the documented position or framework;
2. the application of that framework to the user’s case; and
3. any agent inference.

Never use an expert name as a substitute for evidence, and never fabricate a quotation.
