# PRODUCT-MARKET FIT ENGINE & GROWTH LOOPS

Quantify, optimize, and systematically expand Product-Market Fit (PMF) using Rahul Vohra's Superhuman PMF Engine, and architect compounding, self-sustaining Growth Loops based on Brian Balfour (Reforge) and Andrew Chen.

## Operating Boundary

- **Triggers:** Product-Market Fit engine, Rahul Vohra PMF method, Sean Ellis 40% benchmark, High-Expectation Customer (HXC) segmentation, 50/50 roadmap allocation, Brian Balfour growth loops, Andrew Chen viral loops, loops vs. funnels, compounding acquisition engines, content growth loops, retention loops, viral cycle time.
- **Cross-Domain Scope:** 0-to-1 PMF discovery, 1-to-10 growth acceleration, viral loop engineering, bottom-up product-led growth (PLG), customer retention optimization, organic compounding strategy.
- **Anti-Triggers / Exclusions:** Routine digital marketing ad campaign setup (e.g., Google Ads keyword bidding), pure landing page copywriting (use `conversion-copywriting`), visual checkout UX styling (use `commerce-ux-rules`), or routine bug triage.

## Theoretical Foundations

1. **The Quantitative PMF Engine (Rahul Vohra & Sean Ellis):**
   - The Sean Ellis Benchmark: *"How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?"* ($\ge 40\%$ "Very Disappointed" indicates quantitative PMF).
   - The High-Expectation Customer (HXC): The most discerning target user archetype whose validation defines the core product vector.
   - The 50/50 Roadmap Principle: Product capacity must be strictly partitioned into **50% doubling down on what lovers love** and **50% removing holdbacks for high-potential fence-sitters**.
2. **Growth Loops vs. Linear Funnels (Brian Balfour & Andrew Chen):**
   - Linear funnels (AARRR / Pirate Metrics) suffer from thermodynamic decay: every step loses users, and customer acquisition cost (CAC) inexorably increases.
   - Growth Loops are closed compounding feedback systems where the output of one cohort directly generates the input for the next cohort:
     $$\text{Input} \longrightarrow \text{Action} \longrightarrow \text{Output} \longrightarrow \text{Reinvested Input}$$
3. **Loop Mechanics & Dynamics (Reforge):**
   - **Viral Loops:** User experiences value $\to$ shares/invites peers $\to$ new users onboard.
   - **Content / SEO Loops:** User or programmatic activity creates indexed assets $\to$ discovered via organic search $\to$ converts visitors into content-creating users.
   - **Paid / Reinvestment Loops:** Paying users generate gross margin $\to$ reinvested in customer acquisition channels with $< 12$-month payback $\to$ acquires more paying users.

### 1. Stage 1: Sean Ellis PMF Scoring & HXC Segmentation (Rahul Vohra)
- Run the core PMF survey on users who experienced the core product within the last 14 days:
  $$\text{PMF Score} = \frac{N_{\text{Very Disappointed}}}{N_{\text{Total Respondents}}} \times 100\%$$
- If $\text{PMF Score} < 40\%$, isolate the **High-Expectation Customer (HXC)**:
  - Analyze the demographics and job titles of "Very Disappointed" respondents.
  - Filter out "Somewhat Disappointed" respondents whose use case does *not* match the HXC persona.
  - Recalculate the HXC-adjusted PMF score.

### 2. Stage 2: Feedback Synthesis & Bad-Feedback Pruning
- Group qualitative responses into distinct cohorts:
  - **The Lovers ("Very Disappointed"):** Identify the #1 core benefit. This is your irreplaceable anchor and north star.
  - **The High-Potential Fence-Sitters ("Somewhat Disappointed" who value the core benefit):** Analyze what is holding them back. These holdbacks form your high-yield conversion backlog.
  - **The Distractors ("Somewhat Disappointed" who do not care about the core benefit + "Not Disappointed"):** **DISCARD THEIR FEEDBACK ENTIRELY.** Building for distractors will dilute core product positioning and destroy PMF.

### 3. Stage 3: Construct the 50/50 PMF Roadmap
- Partition engineering/product sprint capacity into exact halves:
  - **50% Love Multipliers:** Enhancing speed, power, and depth of the features that "Very Disappointed" users cherish.
  - **50% Holdback Removers:** Systematically eliminating the top friction points and missing table-stakes features for the HXC fence-sitters.

### 4. Stage 4: Architect Closed-Loop Growth Engines (Brian Balfour)
- Discard linear acquisition funnels and design closed **Growth Loops**:
  - **Collaborative / Viral Loop:** User invites collaborators to complete a shared task $\to$ invited users experience value $\to$ create their own workspaces.
  - **Organic Content Loop:** User generates public artifacts/dashboards $\to$ indexed by search engines $\to$ external visitors view artifact $\to$ sign up to copy/edit.
  - **Paid Reinvestment Loop:** High gross margin LTV $\to$ programmatic ad spend with $< 12$-month CAC payback $\to$ scalable customer acquisition.

### 5. Stage 5: Measure Loop Velocity & Compounding Health
- Calculate the **Viral Coefficient ($K$-Factor)** and **Cycle Time ($T$)**:
  $$K = i \times c \quad (i = \text{invites sent per user}, \, c = \text{conversion rate per invite})$$
- Optimize for reducing Cycle Time ($T$): Shrinking loop duration from 14 days to 4 days produces exponential compounding growth.
- Verify cohort retention curves flatten horizontally before accelerating growth loops.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **The 40% Benchmark Rule:** Never scale paid acquisition loops before achieving $\ge 40\%$ PMF among your target HXC cohort. Scaling a sub-40% product accelerates churn and incinerates capital.
2. **Never Build for Non-HXC Users:** Actively ignore feedback from users who do not value the core product benefit, even if they are vocal.
3. **Strict 50/50 Allocation:** Maintain balanced investment between doubling down on core love (50%) and unblocking fence-sitters (50%). Never allocate 100% to feature catch-up.
4. **Retention Before Acquisition:** Growth loops require a stable, flat retention baseline. Loops applied to a leaking bucket will fail.
5. **Closed Loop Verification:** Every growth mechanism must define how the terminal action of one user directly generates the initial trigger for the next user.
