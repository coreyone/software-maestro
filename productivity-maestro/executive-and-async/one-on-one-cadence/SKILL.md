# HIGH-OUTPUT 1:1 CADENCE & COMMITMENT TRACKING

Prepare, conduct, and debrief high-leverage One-on-One (1:1) meetings based on Andy Grove's *High Output Management*, Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path*, and Kim Scott's *Radical Candor*.

> "A 90-minute 1:1 with a direct report can enhance the quality of your subordinate's work for 2 full weeks, or for some eighty-plus hours. It has enormous managerial leverage." — Andy Grove

---

## Operating Boundary

- **Triggers:** Any request to prepare a 1:1 agenda, structure a manager-direct report conversation, debrief a 1:1 session, track two-way mutual commitments, diagnose motivation/burnout, unblock cross-functional friction, or document long-term career growth.
- **Cross-Disciplinary Standards Applied:**
  - **`technical-language-rules` (ASD-STE100 + Google DevDocs):** Imperative action verbs, $\le 20$ words per commitment item, unambiguous connectors (`because`, `after`, `can`, `must`).
  - **`info-to-ink` (Output Token Compression):** High signal-to-noise ratio. Eliminate administrative fluff and transcript banter. Deliver clean, structured commitment contracts.
  - **`the-elements-of-style-principles` (Strunk & White):** Active voice, positive assertions, and concrete, observable behavioral feedback.
  - **`marketing-copy-emotion-provoking-action-driven`:** Empathetic, motivating framing for career trajectories and leadership development goals.
- **Anti-Triggers / Exclusions:** Routine status updates (status belongs in async tools, not 1:1s), legal HR documentation, or formal termination filings.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Direct Report Context:** Name, role, tenure, current project focus, and past commitments.
2. **Session Mode:**
   - **Mode A: Pre-Meeting Agenda Preparation** (Questions to ask, topics to probe).
   - **Mode B: Post-Meeting Debrief & Commitment Contract** (Agreements reached, mutual action items).
   - **Mode C: Quarterly Career Coaching & Trajectory Calibration** (Skill progression, feedback, growth goals).

---

## The Andy Grove 4-Pillar 1:1 Architecture

The 1:1 belongs primarily to the **direct report**, not the manager. The manager acts as a facilitator, listener, and unblocker across 4 core pillars:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                              THE 4-PILLAR 1:1 ARCHITECTURE                             │
├───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┤
│ Pillar                │ Focus Area                    │ Core Question to Probe         │
├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Energy & Bandwidth │ Psychological safety & morale │ "What energized or drained you?"│
│ 2. Priority Alignment │ Focus & high-leverage work    │ "What is your #1 focus lever?" │
│ 3. Unblocking & Risks │ Friction, cross-team blockers │ "What is slowing you down?"    │
│ 4. Growth & Feedback  │ Radical Candor & trajectory   │ "Where do you want to stretch?"│
└───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Execution Instructions

Follow this 4-stage operational workflow:

```
  [Stage 1: Ingestion & Review] --> Review previous 1:1 commitments and lingering open loops.
             │
  [Stage 2: 4-Pillar Check-In]  --> Probe Energy, Alignment, Friction, and Growth.
             │
  [Stage 3: Two-Way Commitments]--> Separate Direct Report deliverables from Manager promises.
             │
  [Stage 4: Commitment Locking] --> Assign single DRIs, hard deadlines, and check-in dates.
```

### Stage 1: Review Prior Commitments
Check the status of action items agreed upon in the previous 1:1. Did the manager unblock the promised obstacles? Did the direct report complete their milestones?

### Stage 2: Probe Deeply with Powerful Questions
- **Avoid:** *"How are things going?"* (Yields polite, low-signal *"Good"*).
- **Ask:** *"What part of your project felt unnecessarily slow or frustrating this week?"*
- **Ask:** *"If you were CEO for a day, what stupid process would you eliminate immediately?"*
- **Ask:** *"What feedback do you have for me on how I can support your velocity better?"*

### Stage 3: Build the Two-Way Commitment Contract
Every 1:1 must produce a balanced two-way action registry:
1. **Direct Report Actions:** Strategic deliverables and professional stretch goals.
2. **Manager Commitments:** Escalations, cross-team unblocking, resource procurement, and executive shielding.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **The 1:1 Is Not a Status Update:** Never spend the 1:1 reciting task lists available in Jira/GitHub. Focus on sentiment, alignment, obstacles, and coaching.
2. **Mandatory Two-Way Commitments:** Every 1:1 record MUST document commitments from BOTH the direct report and the manager.
3. **Deterministic STE Action Verbs:** Action items must begin with an imperative verb (`Unblock`, `Introduce`, `Deliver`, `Schedule`, `Draft`).
4. **Concrete, Observable Feedback:** Feedback must refer to specific recent events, not vague personality traits (*"In Tuesday's design review, your presentation was clear because..."*).
5. **Strict Single-DRI Enforcement:** Each commitment has one named human owner and a hard date.

---

## Completion Gate

Before finalizing any 1:1 record:
- [ ] All 4 pillars (Energy, Alignment, Unblocking, Growth) are evaluated.
- [ ] Specific obstacles and friction points are documented with manager remediation actions.
- [ ] Two-way commitment table contains deliverables for both Direct Report and Manager.
- [ ] Every action item has a single human DRI, an imperative verb, and a hard deadline.
- [ ] Sentences comply with ASD-STE100 brevity limits ($\le 20$ words per task).
