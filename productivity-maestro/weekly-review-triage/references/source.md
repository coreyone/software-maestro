# Canonical References & Operational Standards: Weekly Review & Task Triage

This document outlines the methodology, psychology of focus, and operational literature underlying weekly task triage and executive prioritization.

---

## 1. Theoretical Foundations

### 1.1 Getting Things Done (GTD) & Mind Sweep (David Allen)
- **Origin:** David Allen (*Getting Things Done: The Art of Stress-Free Productivity*, 2001).
- **The Cognitive Bottleneck:** The human brain is designed for generating ideas, not storing them. Unclosed commitments ("open loops") create subconscious attention residue, degrading executive focus and working memory.
- **The GTD Weekly Review:** The master habit that restores cognitive clarity by collecting, processing, organizing, and reviewing all active commitments every 7 days.

### 1.2 The Eisenhower Decision Matrix (Dwight D. Eisenhower / Stephen Covey)
- **Origin:** General Dwight D. Eisenhower's prioritization heuristic, popularized by Stephen Covey (*The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*, 1989).
- **The Urgency Trap:** What is urgent is seldom important, and what is important is seldom urgent. Reactive engineers live entirely in Quadrants 1 & 3; elite contributors deliberately carve out protected time in Quadrant 2 (Strategic / Non-Urgent Deep Work).

### 1.3 Cal Newport's Deep Work & The Weekly Plan
- **Origin:** Cal Newport (*Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World*, 2016).
- **Context-Switching Tax:** Shifting between shallow communication and deep engineering incurs an exponential attention residue cost. A weekly triage isolates deep work into dedicated, non-negotiable blocks.

---

## 2. Integrated Writing & Execution Standards

- **ASD-STE100 Rules:** Every task begins with an imperative action verb. Procedural task sentences $\le 20$ words.
- **Info-to-Ink:** Omit personal reflections and conversational filler. Deliver high-density, structured markdown tables.
- **Strunk & White Precision:** Positive assertions; concrete deliverables instead of abstract process descriptions.
