# Rule: High-Conversion, Emotion-Provoking & Action-Driven Copywriting

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ethos & Theoretical Lineage**:
> Grounded in **Eugene Schwartz** (*Breakthrough Advertising* - 5 Stages of Awareness), **Joanna Wiebe** (Conversion Copywriting formulas: PAS, AIDA, BAB), **Julian Shapiro** (Clear vs. Clever copywriting), and **Tony Fadell (*Build* — Ch 13: Storytelling & Ch 27: A Method to the Marketing)** (The "On-the-Box" 3-sentence hook: *"Marketing starts on Day 1. Sell the emotional transformation on the retail box before writing code"*).
>
> **The Prime Directive**: *"Enter the conversation already happening in the customer's mind. Sell the transformation, not the features."*

---

## When to use

Use this skill when drafting or optimizing customer-facing messaging and copy:
- Crafting **Tony Fadell "On-the-Box" 3-Sentence Hooks** and Day-1 elevator pitches.
- Writing high-converting **Hero Section Headlines & Subheads** (Clear > Clever).
- Formulating **Value Propositions** anchored in customer struggle and emotional relief.
- Creating high-intent, action-oriented **Call-to-Action (CTA) Button Copy**.
- Crafting feature-to-benefit translation matrices (Feature $\to$ Advantage $\to$ Emotional Benefit).
- Auditing landing page copy for clarity, cognitive friction, and conversion blockers.

## When not to use

Do not use this skill for:
- Writing deterministic technical API documentation (use `technical-language-rules`).
- Crafting internal executive async decision memos (use `executive-async-memo`).
- Engineering A/B test statistical sample sizing (use `experimentation-hypothesis-engine`).

## Trigger cues

- Request mentions: `conversion copywriting`, `marketing copy`, `landing page copy`, `value proposition`, `CTA copy`, `emotional hook`, `headlines`, `benefit copy`, `hero section copy`, `conversion messaging`.

## Completion gate

- [ ] Headline captures target audience pain point in $\le 10$ words with zero corporate jargon.
- [ ] Subhead clearly explains the product mechanism and expected outcome in $\le 20$ words.
- [ ] Primary CTA uses first-person or action-oriented imperative verbs (e.g., *"Start My Free Trial"* instead of *"Submit"*).
- [ ] Copy eliminates passive voice and generic buzzwords (*"seamless", "all-in-one", "next-gen"*).

---

## Anti-Patterns & Copywriting Invariants

Eliminate generated-copy cadence tells, generic SaaS fluff, and rhetorical clichés:

- **`theater-slop-phrase` (Theater framing copy)**: Lazily labeling legacy workflows or alternative tools as *"X theater"* (*"compliance theater"*, *"management theater"*). Clearly articulate concrete trade-offs, measurable latencies, and technical failure modes instead.

Cut the AI garbage but keep my voice, jokes, cadence, bluntness, and rhythm. Edit minimally. Never sanitize, homogenize, or invent facts, quotes, or stats; ask if unclear. Active voice with human subjects. Direct verbs; use is and has over serves as. Specific names, dates, metrics, and mechanisms only. If a sentence fits anywhere else, cut it. Show facts; delete commentary declaring things important, obvious, or subtle. Repeat the right word instead of cycling synonyms. Ban binary contrasts (not X but Y), negative lists, throat-clearing intros, fake insights (what nobody tells you, part everyone misses), colon reveals, and trailing participle clauses (highlighting, underscoring). Kill puffery (pivotal moment, testament to), reader coaching (in other words, that matters because), and weasel sourcing (studies show, experts agree). No dramatic fragments (that is it), staccato cadences, fake-profound mic drops, or recap conclusions (in conclusion, ultimately). End on the last concrete fact or next step. No em dashes in short copy, max two in long drafts. No header emojis, decorative bolding, pointless bullets, or micro-headers. Ban outright: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, this is huge, this changes everything, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness, ever-evolving, next-generation, world-class, seamless, all-in-one, enterprise-grade. Drop filler adverbs (literally, actually, fundamentally, crucially) and dead phrases (at the end of the day, it is worth noting, in today's world, going forward, let us dive in).
