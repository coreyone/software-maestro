# How Might We (HMW) Generative Problem Reframing Framework

## 1. Lineage & Philosophy

```
  Min Basadur (1970s, Procter & Gamble)
  └─ "How might we..." as a semantic unlocking mechanism:
     • "How" implies that solutions exist and can be discovered.
     • "Might" grants permission to generate wild, unconstrained hypotheses.
     • "We" signals collective ownership and cross-functional exploration.
         │
         ▼
  IDEO & Stanford d.school (Tim Brown, David Kelley)
  └─ The 10 Reframing Lenses & The Goldilocks Calibration:
     • Expanding one pain point into multiple diverging vectors.
         │
         ▼
  Jake Knapp (Google Ventures Design Sprint)
  └─ Real-time expert interview note-taking & Decider targeting.
```

---

## 2. The Goldilocks Sweet Spot Matrix

| Calibration Zone | Characteristics | Example | Risk / Flaw |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Too Narrow** (Prescriptive) | Embeds a pre-selected solution, UI element, button, or architecture. | *"How might we build a wizard form with a progress bar for checkout?"* | Kills innovation; teams jump to execution without exploring superior alternatives. |
| **Too Broad** (Ocean Boiling) | Lacks constraints, persona context, or observable friction. | *"How might we fix payments?"* | Paralyzes ideation; too ambiguous to design testable prototypes. |
| **Goldilocks Sweet Spot** | Anchored in user emotional progress and business context, completely open on mechanism. | *"How might we make first-time international checkout feel as trustworthy and effortless as paying in a local storefront?"* | Inspires dozens of diverse UI, financial, and UX solutions. |

## 3. The 10 Stanford d.school / IDEO Reframing Lenses

| # | Lens | Purpose | Reframing Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Amplify the Good** | Leverage what already works. | *"How might we take [positive behavior] and make it the focal point of the entire experience?"* |
| **2** | **Remove the Bad** | Eliminate the ordeal completely. | *"How might we remove the need for [painful step] altogether?"* |
| **3** | **Explore the Opposite** | Turn the problem on its head. | *"How might we make [slow/tedious aspect] deliberately slow and celebrated?"* |
| **4** | **Question an Assumption** | Break an industry sacred cow. | *"How might we deliver [value] without ever asking the user for [assumed requirement]?"* |
| **5** | **Target the Emotion** | Focus on the psychological relief. | *"How might we turn the user's feelings of [anxiety/confusion] into feelings of [mastery/confidence]?"* |
| **6** | **Identify Unexpected Resources** | Leverage ambient data or idle capacity. | *"How might we use [existing background data/community knowledge] to solve this for the user?"* |
| **7** | **Create an Analogy** | Borrow from unrelated domains. | *"How might we treat [boring workflow] like checking in at a 5-star hotel concierge / playing a video game?"* |
| **8** | **Reframe into a Feature** | Turn the constraint into an asset. | *"How might we make [limitation/delay] the primary reason users trust our product?"* |
| **9** | **Shift the Status Quo** | Move burden from human to system. | *"How might we predict and execute [task] automatically before the user even realizes it's needed?"* |
| **10** | **Break into Micro-Slices** | Deconstruct massive friction. | *"How might we break [intimidating 20-minute setup] into zero-friction 5-second interactions?"* |

## 4. Operational Anti-Patterns & Repairs

| Anti-Pattern | Diagnostic Signal | Repair Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solution Smuggling** | The HMW mentions an app, modal, button, AI bot, or notification. | Strip the noun. Ask *"What outcome does that button achieve for the user?"* and reframe around the outcome. |
| **Apathy Generation** | The HMW is so broad that nobody knows what to sketch in Crazy 8s. | Add the specific user persona and emotional friction point. |
| **Single-Lens Fixation** | All generated HMWs are variations of "Remove the Bad". | Force the team to apply all 10 lenses before selecting top candidates. |
